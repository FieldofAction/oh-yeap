Start Here.

How I built my site with Claude, and how you can start yours.

A few friends have asked me how I made my site. This is the honest answer, written down so I can just hand it over. It is not a tutorial. It is the handful of things I wish someone had told me on day one.

01

The only hard part is believing it is hard

Here is how I got started. I asked a friend how he built his thing, and he said the line above. I almost didn't believe him. I had it in my head that this kind of work was supposed to be difficult, that I would need to learn a stack of things first.

Once I let go of that idea, everything opened up. The questions and thoughts I had been sitting on started pointing back at me, ready to use.

You do not need to know how to code. You need to know what you care about. Claude handles the how. You bring the what.

So the first step is small: open a conversation and describe what you want in plain words. Like you would to a friend.

02

Decide what you actually want

Before you build anything, get clear on what the thing is for. This one decision steers everything that comes after.

For me, the goal was specific. I wanted a living archive. A place I could update on a regular basis, put my ideas into, and change whenever I felt like it. A forever site. That is a different goal than a portfolio you finish once and leave alone, and knowing the difference shaped every choice I made.

A few questions worth answering out loud before you start:

  • 01What is this for, in one sentence?
  • 02Who is it for? (It is fine if the answer is "me.")
  • 03Is it something you finish and leave, or something you keep changing?
  • 04A year from now, what do you want to be able to do with it?

Write your answers down. Keep them short. Paste them into your first conversation so the work starts pointed in the right direction.

03

Let your medium lead

The medium you work in most should shape the kind of site you build. A few starting points:

Motion, film, broadcastLet the work play. Lead with autoplaying, muted video loops and full-bleed frames, and keep the interface quiet so the motion is the thing that moves.
Brand and identityShow the system, not only the logo. Big marks, work before and after, and the identity applied across real surfaces, with case studies that walk through the thinking.
Product and digitalShow it working. Embed live demos or prototypes, walk through one real flow, and frame screens in context. Lead with the problem you solved.
Photography and visual workGet out of the image's way. Full-bleed pictures, generous space, minimal type, and a fast lightbox. Let scale and sequence carry it.
Writing and strategyLet type do the work. An index-style, text-forward site with a great reading experience and almost no decoration.
Illustration, graphic, and printShow range and craft. A confident grid, real color, and room for detail. A little playfulness fits here.
Spatial and physical workShow scale and context. Large photography, process shots, and the work in the place it lives.

If you work across a few of these, lead with the one that carries the most weight, and let the site hold the rest quietly.

04

Bring your taste

Claude can build almost anything, which means the taste has to come from you. This is the part I care most about, and it is where I did the most work before I ever touched code.

Ahead of time, I gathered references: screenshots of sites I loved, notes on how I wanted mine to feel, a few documents I had already written about my thinking. Having that material ready got us into the right space fast. Then Claude and I worked closely on the styling, going back and forth, adjusting until it felt like mine.

The tip: write a short style note and share it early. It does not need to be fancy. A page is plenty. There is one you can steal just below.

05

A few things that helped

Just ask, in plain language.Describe the idea the way you would out loud. You do not need the right vocabulary.
Work in small steps.Build a little, look at it, react. Momentum comes from seeing something real quickly.
Add, then subtract.Some of my best decisions were removing things I had just added. Let the simple version win.
Treat it as a conversation.When something feels off, say so. Claude adjusts. "This feels too loud" is a real instruction.
Keep your intentions in a document.A short file with your goals and style notes, one you can hand back anytime, keeps the work consistent.
Aim for something you can keep changing."Done" is a heavy target. A site you can return to and edit is a lighter, better one.
Steal this — a starter style note 
WHAT IT'S FOR
One sentence. What is this, and who is it for?

HOW IT SHOULD FEEL
Three or four words. (Calm? Playful? Serious? Spacious?)

REFERENCES I LOVE
Two or three sites, one line each on what I like.

TYPE & COLOR LEANINGS
Any fonts, colors, or moods I'm drawn to. Rough is fine.

WHAT TO KEEP OUT
Anything I know I don't want.
Steal this — a first message to Claude
I want to build a personal site. It is for [what it's for]. It should feel [three words]. I want to be able to [the thing you want to keep doing with it]. Here are a few references I love, and why. Can we start simple and build it up together?
Steal this — three prompts for a running start

Complete openers for three different tastes. Pick the closest one, paste it in, and adjust as you go.

01The elegant portfolio
I want a beautiful portfolio site for my work. Keep the layout calm and confident, with generous space and strong typography doing most of the work. Add elegant micro-motion: smooth transitions between pages, subtle reveals as sections come into view, and refined hover states on links and project cards. Keep it restrained, so the motion feels expensive and hand-tuned. Start with the home page and one project page, and we'll build from there.
02The quiet index with one surprise
I want a simple index-style site: a clean, text-forward list of my work and writing, mostly type and space, no big images or cards. Then add one unexpected piece of motion whimsy, something small and surprising that rewards attention and makes people smile, while the reading stays easy. Keep everything else quiet so the one playful moment lands. Show me a few ideas for that moment before we build it.
03Expensive through simplicity
I'm a senior creative. I want a hybrid site that holds both polished case studies and smaller one-off projects, and still feels calm. I want it to look expensive, and for that to come from simplicity: restraint, precise spacing, a considered type scale, and one confident accent. Make the case studies feel editorial and the one-offs light and quick to browse. Start with a home page that ties the two together.
What you'll need

A short kit, and two honest paths

You do not need much to start. The main choice is how hands-on you want to be.

ClaudeI used Claude Code, which runs on your own machine and can build and change the files directly. The desktop app and claude.ai work well too, if you would rather chat and copy things across.
Somewhere for it to liveTwo honest paths. To build from scratch and own every detail, pair a code home with a deploy host: I used GitHub and Vercel, both free to start. To skip the code entirely, an all-in-one builder like Squarespace or Framer lets you design and publish in one place.
A domain, when you want oneYour own URL makes it feel like yours, and you can grab one anytime. You do not need it on day one. Every host gives you a free address to begin with, and you can point a custom domain at it later.

That is the whole kit. Pick the path that matches how hands-on you want to be, and you can start today.

The hardest step is the first sentence.

Once you say what you want out loud, the rest starts pointing at you. Have fun with it.
