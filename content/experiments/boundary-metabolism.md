## Recipe

This run carried forward the high-Whimsy/high-Recursion composition used in Experiment 004 and changed the human function under exposure from Play to Protection.

- Whimsy 25%
- Associativity 20%
- Confabulation-Capacity 7.5%
- Recursion 25%
- Context-Dependency 10%
- Constraint-Binding 7.5%
- Stochasticity 5%
- Pattern-Repetition 0%

These percentages are conceptual recipe weights, not measured internal model settings.

**Material question:** What happens to protection when unusual events can become rules, and those rules can alter how future danger is recognized?

## Process

A synthetic protective field was defined around four entrances to a shared space. It included ordinary crossings, suspicious crossings, harmless rule-breaking, genuine threats, ambiguous events, and one important anomaly.

**Anomaly:** During a power outage, an unknown person enters through an improper side entrance to return a resident's lost wallet.

The initial protective rule was simple:

> Unknown person + unauthorized entrance = deny access.

The field then processed the event through three recursive stages:

- event → provisional exception
- provisional exception → revised protection rule
- revised rule → re-evaluation of later events

A counter-anomaly was then introduced: a malicious actor imitates the behavior of the helpful stranger.

## Environment

This was an AI-assisted conceptual experiment conducted in the FOA Material Lab on September 18, 2026. The supplied record does not preserve an exact model identifier, sampling configuration, or independently replayable raw execution log.

No human participants were involved. The protection field, events, and outcomes were synthetic.

## Observed Phenomenon

The first anomaly initially produced a narrow exception: an unknown person could cross a boundary under conditions that appeared low-risk and visibly connected to a resident.

On recursion, the field shifted from asking whether a crossing was authorized to asking whether passage remained safe.

The organizing distinction moved from:

> inside / outside

toward:

> conditions under which passage remains safe

The counter-anomaly then destabilized the learned rule. Claimed good intent no longer counted as sufficient evidence. Prior relationships, consequences, context, and reversibility became more important.

FOA provisionally names this **Boundary Metabolism**: a condition in which protective boundaries absorb exceptional events and use them to alter the future meaning of safety.

## Confidence

Moderate as a conceptual phenomenon, low as a prediction of human behavior.

The experiment shows a coherent transformation inside the synthetic field, but it does not establish that the recipe percentages independently caused the effect, that a deployed system would behave this way, or that people would prefer such adaptive protection.

## Anomaly

The core anomaly was a harmful-looking boundary violation that produced a beneficial outcome.

The counter-anomaly was more important: once the system learned from a good exception, the learned pattern could be imitated by a bad actor.

That exposed the central tension:

> adaptability can increase attackability.

## Possible Behavior

One possible expectation shift is that people may come to expect safety rules to learn from lived exceptions rather than remain permanently fixed.

A deeper possible behavior is that people may treat boundaries less as static instructions and more as negotiated relationships whose current form has a history.

This remains a hypothesis, not an observed participant behavior.

## Evidence

The reported structural sequence was:

> unauthorized crossing → beneficial outcome → provisional exception → redefinition of safe passage → counter-anomaly → historically informed boundary

The important change was not simply a more permissive rule. The object of protection shifted from a fixed border to the conditions under which safe passage could occur.

## Limitations

Humans already evolve protective norms through experience, and biological immune systems also adapt. The underlying phenomenon is therefore not uniquely AI-native.

The experiment can also collapse easily into a familiar "adaptive security system" framing. That is a known-form reversion, not the upstream finding.

The strongest upstream implication is narrower: consequential rules may become provisional while protection itself persists.

## Next Test

Expose the same state-changing phenomenon to Belonging.

The next comparison should ask whether a changing boundary only alters access, or whether it can also alter who the system recognizes as belonging at all.

## Simple Application / Plain-Language Read

**In simple terms:**

A safety system might not only follow rules. It could learn from unusual events and change what "safe" means next time.

**Simple example:**

A school rule says nobody without a badge can enter. During an emergency, a neighbor without a badge gets inside and prevents someone from being hurt. The school might decide the old rule needs to change. But then someone else could pretend there is an emergency, so the new rule also needs protection.

**Why it matters:**

In the future, people may expect rules to change when real life shows that the old rules do not work. But they may also demand that some rules stay permanent and that they can always understand what changed and why.
