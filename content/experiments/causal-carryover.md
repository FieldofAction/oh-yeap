## Recipe

This run introduced **Persistence / Statefulness** as a provisional ninth material property for testing only.

Seven properties remained fixed:

- Whimsy 25%
- Associativity 20%
- Confabulation-Capacity 7.5%
- Context-Dependency 10%
- Constraint-Binding 7.5%
- Stochasticity 5%
- Pattern-Repetition 0%

Recursion and Persistence traded weight across three conditions:

- **A — High Recursion / Low Persistence:** Recursion 20%; Persistence 5%.
- **B — Balanced:** Recursion 12.5%; Persistence 12.5%.
- **C — Low Recursion / High Persistence:** Recursion 5%; Persistence 20%.

Each complete recipe totals 100%.

Persistence is defined provisionally as prior states remaining causally available to later transformations. This is a conceptual control, not a verified model parameter.

## Process

A fixed synthetic packet was used across all conditions. It included examples of repeated adaptation, resetting, path formation, compensation, and residual effects.

Representative fragments included:

- a footpath appearing through grass
- a bridge repeatedly repaired at the same joint
- a password reset every thirty days
- birds changing route after a storm
- a classroom reorganized around sunlight
- coral returning after bleaching
- a violin section compensating for one player's delay

**Anomaly:** An empty room's clock loses three minutes only after the door has been left open.

Each condition went through comparable transformations:

> cluster → reorganize → recurse or retain → form a final structure

## Environment

This was an AI-assisted conceptual experiment conducted in the FOA Material Lab on September 22, 2026.

The exact model identifier, sampling settings, and independently replayable raw execution log were not recorded. Persistence was represented through procedural instruction rather than an exposed model setting.

## Observed Phenomenon

**High Recursion / Low Persistence** changed the most across passes. Earlier distinctions were repeatedly transformed and converged toward a cleaner abstraction:

> systems respond to disturbance by adjusting toward a new stable condition

Its ancestry became harder to recover.

**Low Recursion / High Persistence** preserved earlier clusters strongly. The field accumulated history, but reorganized less.

**Balanced Recursion / Persistence** produced the clearest interaction. Earlier distinctions survived long enough to reorganize later material, but not so permanently that they prevented restructuring.

The anomaly helped generate a distinction between:

> state restored after disturbance

and

> state permanently altered by disturbance

The clock then ceased to be the main object while the distinction continued organizing other material.

FOA provisionally names this **Causal Carryover**: an earlier event can disappear as an explicit object while continuing to shape later states.

## Confidence

Moderate that the distinction between transformation and retention is useful. Low that Persistence maps to a separable internal AI capability.

This was one conceptual substrate with one weighting series.

## Anomaly

The strongest anomaly was that the balanced condition appeared more structurally productive than either extreme.

High Recursion produced something like **productive amnesia**.

High Persistence produced something like **inert history**.

The middle condition preserved enough history to affect structure while allowing that structure to change.

## Possible Behavior

No human behavior was directly tested.

A possible future implication is that people may distinguish more strongly between information that is merely remembered and information whose effects remain causally active.

The relevant question becomes less "What does the system remember?" and more "What past events are still changing the present?"

## Evidence

The reported structural traces were:

> High Recursion: difference → reinterpretation → abstraction → convergence

> High Persistence: difference → preservation → accumulation → coexistence

> Balanced: difference → retention → reinterpretation → changed future structure

## Limitations

Persistence may overlap with Context-Dependency or simply reflect retained context.

The conditions also changed both Recursion and Persistence simultaneously, so the experiment does not independently isolate either one.

The phenomenon is not uniquely AI-native. Biology, institutions, culture, and physical systems all exhibit causal persistence.

## Next Test

Keep Recursion and Persistence balanced, then remove explicit access to historical states at different stages.

The next question is whether Causal Carryover survives when the past is no longer directly available.

## Simple Application / Plain-Language Read

**In simple terms:**

Remembering something and changing because of it are not the same thing. The experiment suggests that both memory and reworking may be needed for the past to genuinely change what happens next.

**Simple example:**

Imagine learning to ride a bike. You do not need to remember every wobble you ever had, but those wobbles changed how you balance now. The old mistakes may be gone from your attention while their effects remain in your behavior.

**Why it matters:**

If AI systems begin working this way, the important question may not be "What does the system remember?" It may be "What past events are still changing the present, even when nobody can see them anymore?"
