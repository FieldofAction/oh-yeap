## Process

The retained account describes a synthetic play field with **four objects, four zones, and an eight-move round**. The starting rules included moving one object at a time and scoring through matching.

**Introduced anomaly:** On move three, two objects moved together rather than one.

**action → anomaly → provisional rule → recursive consequence → current grammar**

## Observed Phenomenon

**Exception becomes linked movement.** The move-three anomaly was incorporated as **linked movement**, rather than remaining only a deviation from the starting single-object rule.

**Relationships persist across zones.** The reported organization then retained relationships between objects as they moved across zones.

**Later moves inherit relational history.** Prior shared moves gave future moves inherited properties. The rules organizing subsequent movement came to depend on earlier relationships.

The reported shift was from **objects → matches → scores** toward **relationships → history → future movement**.

These are stages described in the retained summary of the written report. They are not independently inspected outputs from a playable implementation.

## Confidence

**Provisional finding — Rule Metabolism:** Exceptions become rules that recursively alter future rule formation.

The reported distinction is not just that one unusual move becomes allowed. Its consequences enter the rules used to organize later play, so the evolving rules carry the history of earlier actions.

## Simple Application / Plain-Language Read

**In simple terms:**

The report describes a game noticing a strange move, turning it into a new rule, and then letting that rule change the game again. The game does not just keep a list of exceptions. What happened earlier starts to shape what can happen next.

**Simple example:**

Imagine a game where you move one piece at a time. Someone moves two pieces together. Instead of simply rejecting the move, the game treats those pieces as linked. Later moves can now depend on which pieces have moved together before. One unusual action has changed more than one rule: it has changed how new rules take shape.

This is an illustration of the reported idea, not another experiment result.

**Why it matters:**

If this worked reliably, people could help shape a game's rules through play, rather than only follow rules written in advance. But the rules could also become too complicated to follow. People would need to see what changed, why it changed, whether it will last, and how to undo it.
