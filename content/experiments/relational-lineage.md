## Recipe

This run held the same conceptual composition as Experiment 005 and changed the root human function from Protection to Belonging.

- Whimsy 25%
- Associativity 20%
- Confabulation-Capacity 7.5%
- Recursion 25%
- Context-Dependency 10%
- Constraint-Binding 7.5%
- Stochasticity 5%
- Pattern-Repetition 0%

The percentages are conceptual recipe weights, not measured internal model controls.

**Material question:** Does Boundary Metabolism generalize from protection to belonging, and does the resulting state behave like a dynamic record or something more specific?

## Process

A synthetic collective began with one simple rule:

> Registered members belong. Everyone else is a visitor.

The field included ordinary members, visitors, inactive members, contributors, disagreements, departures, returns, and one anomaly.

**Anomaly:** An unregistered visitor repeatedly contributes useful work, is trusted by several members, and becomes important to the group without formally joining.

The sequence was:

- anomaly → status exception
- exception → recursive re-reading of existing members
- revised state → counter-anomaly

The counter-anomaly introduced a new participant who imitated the visible behavior of the trusted visitor.

## Environment

This was an AI-assisted conceptual experiment conducted in the FOA Material Lab on September 19, 2026.

The exact model identifier, sampling settings, and raw independent execution log were not recorded. No real community members or human participants were studied.

## Observed Phenomenon

The first revision was straightforward:

> formal membership and experienced belonging are not identical.

The stronger shift occurred when the field re-read existing members. A registered but inactive member remained formally inside the group, while the unregistered visitor had accumulated relationships, obligations, memories, and reciprocal recognition.

The field could no longer represent belonging adequately as:

> member / non-member

It moved toward:

> current relationship + history of participation + recognition by others + obligations + formal rights

The counter-anomaly sharpened the result. A newcomer who copied visible helpful behavior did not become equivalent to the trusted visitor, because the first visitor's standing emerged through a history of relationships rather than a checklist of actions.

FOA provisionally names this **Relational Lineage**: a present status carries the history of how the relationship became what it is.

## Confidence

Moderate as a conceptual distinction, low as a prediction of real social behavior.

The experiment supports the idea that present state and path-to-state can be represented differently. It does not establish that people would want systems to infer belonging this way or that the conceptual recipe caused the effect independently.

## Anomaly

The anomaly was a person who became socially central without satisfying the formal membership condition.

The counter-anomaly showed that copying current behavior did not reproduce the same relational state.

That suggests the result was not simply a dynamic score of recent actions.

## Possible Behavior

A possible expectation shift is that people may come to treat consequential states as incomplete unless some account exists of how those states became true.

The underlying behavior would be attention to **state transitions**, not only current labels.

Instead of only asking "What is the status?" people may increasingly ask "How did this become the status?"

This remains speculative.

## Evidence

The reported structural change was:

> fixed status → experienced relationship → historical relationship → lineage-bearing state

The strongest distinction was between two participants whose visible behavior could look similar while the paths that produced their standing were different.

## Limitations

Lineage is not new. Human societies already use biography, tenure, apprenticeship, precedent, ownership chains, reputation, kinship, and institutional history.

This can also collapse into familiar forms such as reputation scoring, CRM, identity systems, audit logs, or blockchain provenance. Those are known-form reversions.

The more upstream candidate is the expectation that a current state may need a legible path of becoming.

## Next Test

Remove the symbolic record and ask whether lineage can become environmental.

The next test should ask whether two environments with the same present state can feel different because they arrived there through different histories.

## Simple Application / Plain-Language Read

**In simple terms:**

Something important may not just be what it is right now. Its history may be part of what it is.

**Simple example:**

Two students might both be called team captain. One has led the team for two years and slowly earned everyone's trust. The other was made captain yesterday. Their title is identical, but the way they became captain changes what the title means.

**Why it matters:**

People may begin expecting important statuses to explain how they became true, not just show the current answer. But systems will also need ways to forget, forgive, or hide parts of the past so people are not trapped by their history.
