# Start Here

**How I built my site with Claude, and how you can start yours.**

A few friends have asked me how I made my site. This is the honest answer, written down so I can just hand it over. It is not a tutorial. It is the handful of things I wish someone had told me on day one.

---

## 1. The only hard part is believing it is hard

Here is how I got started. I asked a friend how he built his thing. He said, "I just asked Claude. It's really simple."

I almost didn't believe him. I had it in my head that this kind of work was supposed to be difficult, that I would need to learn a stack of things first. Once I let go of that idea, everything opened up. The questions and thoughts I had been sitting on started pointing back at me, ready to use.

You do not need to know how to code. You need to know what you care about. Claude handles the how. You bring the what.

So the first step is small: open a conversation and describe what you want in plain words. Like you would to a friend.

---

## 2. Decide what you actually want

Before you build anything, get clear on what the thing is for. This one decision steers everything that comes after.

For me, the goal was specific. I wanted a living archive. A place I could update on a regular basis, put my ideas, and change whenever I felt like it. A forever site. That is a different goal than a portfolio that you finish once and leave alone, and knowing the difference shaped every choice I made.

A few questions worth answering out loud before you start:

- What is this for, in one sentence?
- Who is it for? (It is fine if the answer is "me.")
- Is it something you finish and leave, or something you keep changing?
- A year from now, what do you want to be able to do with it?

Write your answers down. Keep them short. Paste them into your first conversation with Claude so the work starts pointed in the right direction.

---

## 3. Let your medium lead

The medium you work in most should shape the kind of site you build. A few starting points:

- **Motion, film, broadcast.** Let the work play. Lead with autoplaying, muted video loops and full-bleed frames, and keep the interface quiet so the motion is the thing that moves.
- **Brand and identity.** Show the system, not only the logo. Big marks, work before and after, and the identity applied across real surfaces, with case studies that walk through the thinking.
- **Product and digital.** Show it working. Embed live demos or prototypes, walk through one real flow, and frame screens in context. Lead with the problem you solved.
- **Photography and visual work.** Get out of the image's way. Full-bleed pictures, generous space, minimal type, and a fast lightbox. Let scale and sequence carry it.
- **Writing and strategy.** Let type do the work. An index-style, text-forward site with a great reading experience and almost no decoration.
- **Illustration, graphic, and print.** Show range and craft. A confident grid, real color, and room for detail. A little playfulness fits here.
- **Spatial and physical work.** Show scale and context. Large photography, process shots, and the work in the place it lives.

If you work across a few of these, lead with the one that carries the most weight, and let the site hold the rest quietly.

## 4. Bring your taste

Claude can build almost anything, which means the taste has to come from you. This is the part I care most about, and it is where I did the most work before I ever touched code.

Ahead of time, I gathered references: screenshots of sites I loved, notes on how I wanted mine to feel, a few documents I had already written about my thinking. Having that material ready got us into the right space fast. Then Claude and I worked closely on the styling, going back and forth, adjusting until it felt like mine.

The tip: write a short style note and share it early. It does not need to be fancy. A page is plenty. There is a template at the bottom of this doc you can steal.

---

## 5. A few things that helped

- **Just ask, in plain language.** Describe the idea the way you would out loud. You do not need the right vocabulary.
- **Work in small steps.** Build a little, look at it, react. Momentum comes from seeing something real quickly.
- **Add, then subtract.** Some of my best decisions were removing things I had just added. Let the simple version win.
- **Treat it as a conversation.** When something feels off, say so. Claude adjusts. "This feels too loud" is a real instruction.
- **Keep your intentions in a document.** A short file with your goals and style notes that you can hand back anytime keeps the work consistent.
- **Aim for something you can keep changing.** "Done" is a heavy target. A site you can return to and edit is a lighter, better one.

## Steal this: a starter style note

Copy this, fill it in, and paste it into your first conversation.

```
WHAT IT'S FOR
One sentence. What is this, and who is it for?

HOW IT SHOULD FEEL
Three or four words. (Calm? Playful? Serious? Spacious? Dense?)

REFERENCES I LOVE
Two or three sites, and one line each on what I like about them.

TYPE & COLOR LEANINGS
Any fonts, colors, or moods I'm drawn to. Rough is fine.

WHAT TO KEEP OUT
Anything I know I don't want.
```

---

## Steal this: a first message to Claude

You can start with something as simple as this:

> I want to build a personal site. It is for [what it's for]. It should feel [three words].
> I want to be able to [the thing you want to keep doing with it]. Here are a few references
> I love and why. Can we start simple and build it up together?

That is genuinely enough to begin.

## Steal this: three prompts for a running start

Complete openers for three different tastes. Pick the closest one, paste it in, and adjust as you go.

**01 · The elegant portfolio**

> I want a beautiful portfolio site for my work. Keep the layout calm and confident, with generous space and strong typography doing most of the work. Add elegant micro-motion: smooth transitions between pages, subtle reveals as sections come into view, and refined hover states on links and project cards. Keep it restrained, so the motion feels expensive and hand-tuned. Start with the home page and one project page, and we'll build from there.

**02 · The quiet index with one surprise**

> I want a simple index-style site: a clean, text-forward list of my work and writing, mostly type and space, no big images or cards. Then add one unexpected piece of motion whimsy, something small and surprising that rewards attention and makes people smile, while the reading stays easy. Keep everything else quiet so the one playful moment lands. Show me a few ideas for that moment before we build it.

**03 · Expensive through simplicity**

> I'm a senior creative. I want a hybrid site that holds both polished case studies and smaller one-off projects, and still feels calm. I want it to look expensive, and for that to come from simplicity: restraint, precise spacing, a considered type scale, and one confident accent. Make the case studies feel editorial and the one-offs light and quick to browse. Start with a home page that ties the two together.

---

The hardest step is the first sentence. Once you say what you want out loud, the rest starts pointing at you. Have fun with it.
