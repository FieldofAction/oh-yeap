# Living Brand: investigation and first build

November 2026. Working names throughout. All brand data in the build is fictional.

## 1. Interpretation

The incumbents are building **brand enforcement for production at scale**.
Typeface, Frontify and Adobe each turn guidelines, and increasingly approvals
and performance, into rules that agents apply. That is valuable and largely
solved for enterprises that will fund an integration.

The product described in the brief does something else. It is **brand
judgment for a person entering a company**. That person has to answer four
questions quickly and honestly:

- What is this company trying to become?
- What has it actually made, and in what circumstances?
- What deserves to influence the next piece of work?
- Who gets to decide?

Then they leave behind something the company can keep.

### The organizing principle

**Context and standing travel with every piece of material, and standing
changes only by a recorded decision.**

- **Context** is what the work was for: type, element, canvas, audience,
  intended outcome, level, dates, exposure and constraints.
- **Standing** is four separate answers for a given purpose:
  - Is it relevant?
  - Is it authorized?
  - Is it reusable here?
  - Is it authoritative?

  Every item also has a class: canon, precedent, contextual success,
  experiment, operational material, or learning archive.

Four rules follow from this:

- Retrieval, reuse, popularity and model output cannot raise standing.
- Only a named person with authority can raise it.
- That decision keeps its evidence, scope, effective date, and what it
  replaced.
- Personalization reorders the view and never edits the record.

The incumbents fold learning back into the rules automatically: Adobe through
"decision traces", Typeface through Arc Loop into Arc Graph. That is where
the risks you listed under recursion come from. This principle is the
counter-position. It is also the clearest defensible difference.

## 2. The nested structure

```
Practitioner workbench  (owned by the person; portable)
  methods · preferences · engagement history
  │
  ├── Engagement: Fernway  (bounded: purpose, role, grant, dates, ending)
  │     choices · proposals · brief · context packages · handoff
  │     │  opens a scoped, revocable window into ↓
  │     ▼
  │   Fernway living brand system  (owned by the company; persists)
  │     material · rules · claims · facts · rights · decisions · authority
  │     └── Brand intelligence: retrieval, standing, tensions, proposals
  │          (reads the system; changes nothing on its own)
  │
  └── Engagement: Brightloop  (ended; grant revoked; separate partition)

Agency workspace = many engagements + a coordination view (dates, people) across them
Enterprise workspace = many brands/units in one company; authority map per brand
Temporary conversational engagement = an engagement started from conversation and
  a few materials, before any system of record is connected
```

The agency and enterprise editions differ in **who owns the system and who
holds authority**, not in how many brands they hold:

- An agency owns its workbench and its engagements. Each client owns its
  own system.
- An enterprise owns the systems and the authority map. Its people and
  partners receive grants into them.

### The distinctions in your brief, checked against what was found

- **Temporary entry with minimal setup: open.**
  - Every incumbent assumes it is the company's system of record and is
    configured in advance.
  - Adobe needs paid provisioning.
- **A portable workbench nested inside company-owned systems: open.**
  - External people get guest seats inside one tenant.
  - The MCP servers make nesting technically possible, but they are pipes,
    not a workbench.
- **Judging what deserves lasting influence: partly covered, and the main
  gap.** Adobe and Typeface learn from approvals and results. None documents
  any of the following:

- **Inquiry beyond campaigns (offer, pricing, service): open between

- **Useful before integration: open.** Adobe and Typeface are built the
  opposite way.
- **Agency client isolation: undocumented** in all three. Finding nothing is
  not proof of absence; re-check once those hosts are reachable.

## 4. Integrate, build or defer

**Integrate** with what is already solved, and only to read from it:

- Guidelines and assets: Frontify MCP, Bynder MCP, Figma MCP.
- Rules from Typeface Arc Graph, where a client already runs it.
- Files from Drive, Box and Notion.
- Permission-aware retrieval, using Glean where present.
- Generation and production: Figma, Adobe, FLORA, Canva.

An engagement should never write to these systems or publish through them.

**Build**, because this is the product:

- The standing model and its four answers.
- Authority maps and the proposal and decision lifecycle, with history.
- The engagement as a bounded object: grant, expiry, revocation, ending.
- Tenant-partitioned retrieval and exports.
- Contextual evaluation: intended impact, observed outcome, contribution and
  transfer, kept separate.
- Carry-forward analysis against a changed brief.
- Scoped context packages for partners and agents.
- The engagement handoff package.
- The portable workbench, which carries methods only.

**Defer**:

- Broad crawling and visual semantic search: start with selected folders.
- Scheduled organizational awareness: design the trigger model first.
- Agent-to-agent negotiation.
- Commercial models for reuse licensing: these need legal validation.
- Live performance ingestion.

## 5. The first experience in this build

This was the candidate in your brief, and I kept it. The sharpest moment is
the question you posed: *"We did something similar last year, but this year's
goals are different. What carries forward?"* It is where context and standing
earn their keep, and no incumbent answers it, so the experience is organized
around it.

**Where it lives:** `/living-brand`, a standalone, unlinked, `noindex` page.

**The engagement it shows.** A freelance creative director enters Fernway, a
fictional family streaming service, to bring lapsed subscribers back. Last
year's *Small Hours* campaign targeted strangers. The workbench then:

- **Orients from a few materials.** Intent, canon in force, what holds (each
  point cited), and how far each file has been understood: discovered,
  extracted, interpreted, verified, approved.
- **Investigates the actual work.** A drawn archive in which each piece shows
  its context, its four standing answers, evidence split into intended,
  observed, contribution and transfer, plus rights, relations and reuse.
- **Interprets** what carries forward:
  - **Preserve** the chime.
  - **Investigate first** the paper-cut hero: recognition is worth keeping,
    but the licence covers paid media only and ends 31 December 2026.
  - **Reverse** the "Discover" copy.
  - **Retire** the "Ad-free, always" end card.
- **Surfaces tensions**:
  - contradictory rules in force
  - drift from canon, including a model-written prompt
  - stale claims
  - an unsupported claim
  - unknown rights
  - attribution not established
  - missing evidence
- **Raises an offer-level hypothesis** (price, not campaign), with its
  uncertainty, a test, and the owner who decides it.
- **Records choices.** Those needing authority are routed to the brand
  steward, counsel or the chief executive.
- **Builds a brief.** Evaluation expectations are set before the work, and
  sources are frozen with fingerprints.
- **Issues a context package** to a landing-page agent: read-only,
  rights-checked, expiring and revocable.
- **Exports the engagement package** the company keeps, and the workbench
  the practitioner keeps (methods only).

**Other viewers:**

- **Brand steward**: adopt, revise, reject or retire, with history.
- **Chief executive**: what is routed to them; an attempt to adopt visual
  canon is refused.
- **External agent**: only its package.
- **Agency lead**: two competing clients shown as dates and people only.

## 6. Tests and an honest account

### Tests

`npm run living-brand:test` runs 22 domain tests. They also run in
`prebuild`. They cover:

- **Wrong-client retrieval**:
  - shared vocabulary
  - a grant that does not match its brand
  - a revoked or expired grant
  - a foreign record smuggled into an index
  - Brightloop markers absent from the brief, the handoff and the package
- **Agency coordination** reveals no strategy.
- **The portable workbench** carries no client material.
- **The four standing answers** stay separate, including that public is not
  permission, and that a licence lapsing mid-flight blocks reuse.
- **Tensions**: each kind, plus time-sensitivity. A claim is not stale
  before the fact that contradicts it.
- **Carry-forward verdicts.**
- **Offer hypotheses** carry an owner and stated uncertainty.
- **Accidental promotion**:
  - 25 reuses change nothing
  - a canon proposal without evidence carries warnings and changes nothing
- **Authority**:
  - the practitioner cannot adopt
  - the chief executive cannot adopt visual canon
  - the steward can, and history is kept
  - double decisions are refused
- **Grants**: a read-only grant cannot propose.
- **Retirement** requires authority and keeps history.
- **Views** never mutate.
- **The brief**: evaluation is set before the work, and fingerprints change
  when a source changes.
- **Routing**: choices are routed, not enacted.
- **Package** scope, expiry and revocation.
- **The handoff** records what was not completed.
- **Orientation** works with only three files, and drops strengths whose
  sources are absent.

`npm run text:test` now covers three workbench states at both widths and in
both themes. It finds 0 violations.

An independent operator test was also run with Playwright, covering each
role. Its results are summarized in the session report.

### What works

- The domain model and every behaviour listed under Tests.
- Persistence in browser storage.
- JSON exports.

### What is simulated

- **Interpretation is deterministic and authored.**
  - The carry-forward rules and both hypotheses are written for this dataset.
  - Strengths are the practitioner's authored interpretation, with
    citations.
  - No model is called.
- **Ingestion.** Material arrives as hand-written records. There is no
  extraction from real files.
- **Multi-tenancy.** It is an in-memory partition: a deep copy of one brand,
  re-checked at query time. Real isolation needs separate indexes and
  storage, per-tenant credentials, per-tenant model contexts, and separated
  logs and exports. The code marks where each would go.
- **Roles.** The viewer switch stands in for identity. There is no sign-in
  or membership.

### What remains

- Real connectors, read-only first: Frontify, Figma, Drive.
- A model-backed interpretation layer that proposes. It must never decide,
  and must be evaluated against the deterministic tests.
- Extraction and duplicate detection.
- Scheduled reviews, such as stale claims and expiring licences, as
  explicit triggers.
- Server persistence with revision checks.
- Visual retrieval.
- Your ChatGPT Sites prototype was not inspected; no export was available.

### Operating costs, as an order of magnitude to validate

- Model inference per engagement: dollars to tens of dollars, depending on
  how much material is read.
- Storage: small, unless previews and video are kept.
- Vendor seats where an integration needs them: Frontify, Figma writes,
  Typeface or Adobe are enterprise contracts.
- Maintenance of the authority map is human time, and it is the real cost.

## 7. Decisions that need you

1. **Who is the first buyer?** The freelancer or agency lead who carries the
   workbench, or the company that owns the system? The build favours the
   practitioner entering a company. That changes pricing, data custody and
   who signs the grant.
2. **Integrate or stand alone at the company end?** One route is to
   position on top of Frontify, Typeface or Adobe, reading them as the system
   of record. The other is to hold the company's knowledge for smaller
   companies that have none. The first is faster and narrower; the second
   competes with the incumbents.
3. **How far should model interpretation go?** It could propose standing
   changes, or be limited to retrieval and summarizing, with every proposal
   written by a person. The principle allows the first only if proposals
   stay labelled as such and never auto-adopt.
4. **Who owns the handoff package and the practitioner's methods?** Here the
   company keeps the package and the practitioner keeps general methods.
   Agencies may want reuse licences for tools such as the Story Builder.
   This needs legal input before it becomes a feature.
